EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
In modern retail and food service environments, traditional manual halal verification frequently introduces consumer friction, resulting in purchasing uncertainty, prolonged ingredient review times, and regular authenticity doubts for the Muslim community. Furthermore, existing consumer methods, typically relying on manual dictionary lookups or text-only search engines, are time-consuming, restricted in scope, and prone to inaccurate compliance detection when complex chemical designations or fraudulent logos are evaluated. To mitigate these real-world consumer vulnerabilities, this capstone project presents HALALVERIFY: A Computer Vision System for Real-Time Ingredient Additive Parsing and Halal Logo Authentication.
HalalVerify modernizes consumer safety by combining mobile smartphone cameras with deep learning architectures to execute automated visual inspection. Operating via an edge-optimized verification pipeline, the system evaluates captured images to instantly parse textual attributes, localize accredited markings, and classify product compliance into distinct states: Halal, Haram, or Doubtful/Unverified. To ensure strict data privacy, image processing for feature extraction occurs strictly within local volatile memory (RAM); no raw camera frames, video feeds, or personal user identity signatures are ever saved to permanent storage or transmitted without authorization.
The system delivers comprehensive features, including a Label Scanner and Parser powered by a custom-trained YOLOv8-Nano object detection model for Halal logo localization, alongside EasyOCR for parsing alphanumeric ingredient text and chemical E-numbers. Parsed strings are dynamically cross-referenced against a custom Supabase relational database backend containing categorized additive safety profiles. Furthermore, HalalVerify includes an interactive Establishment Search Index that transforms regional retail listings into actionable geolocation data, such as automated compliance standing reports, active registration statuses, and verified inventory manifests. These tools enable consumers, retailers, and regulatory inspectors to optimize product verification and audit workflows based on data-driven deep learning models rather than manual lookup methods.
Development was governed by the Agile methodology, ensuring progressive software iterations and robust system performance. Quantitative system performance was rigorously benchmarked using standard computer vision evaluation parameters, specifically Precision, Recall, mean Average Precision (), and Character Error Rate (CER), measured before and after model fine-tuning under varying image conditions. User acceptance and overall system utility were further validated against the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality standards and the System Usability Scale (SUS), ensuring the software meets the operational and cognitive needs of consumer cohorts and regulatory inspectors.
Ultimately, HalalVerify establishes a highly scalable, edge-optimized infrastructure for smart consumer protection in the regional retail and food service sectors. The successful deployment of this system contributes to SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure) and SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production) by reducing verification friction, mitigating exposure to non-compliant ingredients through database-driven transparency, and significantly elevating the overall safety experience for all consumers, including target cohorts within Zamboanga City.

ABSTRACT
Consumer compliance verification remains a persistent friction point for Muslim communities in globalized retail environments, where complex ingredient chemical designations and unverified logos frequently complicate purchasing choices. This capstone project developed HalalVerify, an intelligent computer vision system engineered as an installable Progressive Web Application (PWA) to automate consumer safety workflows via deep learning. Operating through a dual-stream computer vision pipeline, the platform utilizes a fine-tuned YOLOv8-Nano model to capture and authenticate certified Halal markings on packaging while utilizing EasyOCR to parse raw text strings and cross-reference obscure food additives against a custom Supabase relational backend engine. Additionally, the platform incorporates a dedicated Establishment Search Index allowing users to query local markets and dining locations within Zamboanga City for verified compliance transparency. To protect user privacy, image processing occurs strictly inside local volatile memory layers without retaining facial profiles or permanent footage files. Technical benchmark trials quantified model efficiency via Precision, Recall, mean Average Precision (), and Character Error Rate (CER) scales, while subjective system validation was conducted using the System Usability Scale (SUS). The resulting metrics confirm that edge-optimized computer vision systems present a fast, scalable, and independent alternative to restricted commercial APIs, significantly driving consumer transparency and digital infrastructure automation.
Keywords: Computer Vision, YOLOv8-Nano, EasyOCR, Halal Logo Authentication, Ingredient Additive Parsing, Establishment Search.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
Project Context
The increasing availability of imported and locally manufactured food products has provided consumers with a wider variety of choices in supermarkets, convenience stores, and restaurants. However, for Muslim consumers, ensuring that food products comply with halal requirements remains a significant challenge. Although many products display halal certification logos, consumers often find it difficult to verify whether these certifications are authentic, recognized, or issued by accredited halal certifying bodies. Furthermore, ingredient lists frequently contain scientific names, complex chemical designations, or E-numbers that are unfamiliar to ordinary consumers, making it difficult to determine whether a product complies with halal dietary regulations. These challenges create uncertainty and reduce consumer confidence when purchasing packaged food products.
Existing halal verification methods primarily depend on manual inspection of halal logos, barcode scanning applications, QR code verification, or searching through online databases. While these approaches provide useful information, they often require internet connectivity, manual searching, or access to official registries. Additionally, counterfeit or improperly displayed halal logos cannot easily be identified by consumers through traditional verification methods. As food products become increasingly diverse and globalized, there is a growing need for a faster, more intelligent, and user-friendly verification system that can assist consumers in making informed purchasing decisions.
Furthermore, a significant operational gap exists regarding structural lookup accessibility for local food businesses. Consumers inside a specific municipality often lack public methods to check if an establishment maintains an active compliance certification or hosts specific certified products. Integrating spatial queries with label analysis addresses this transparency gap.
Recent advancements in Artificial Intelligence (AI), Computer Vision, Optical Character Recognition (OCR), and Deep Learning have enabled the development of intelligent image-based verification systems capable of recognizing objects, extracting textual information, and validating compliance data. Object detection models such as YOLOv8-Nano provide accurate real-time detection of certification logos, while OCR engines such as EasyOCR enable automated extraction of ingredient lists and E-numbers from captured images. These technologies provide an opportunity to automate halal verification without requiring specialized hardware or extensive manual intervention.
To address these challenges, the proponents propose HalalVerify: A Computer Vision System for Real-Time Ingredient Additive Parsing and Halal Logo Authentication. The proposed system is a Progressive Web Application (PWA) that utilizes a multi-stage computer vision structure. The first module detects accredited halal certification logos on product packaging using YOLOv8-Nano. The second module extracts raw ingredient text using EasyOCR and cross-references parsed food additives against a custom Supabase relational database backend containing categorized compliance profiles. Finally, the system introduces a dedicated establishment query index to verify the legal status of local food hubs, providing consumers, retailers, and regulatory personnel with a fast, accessible, and intelligent tool for halal verification that promotes consumer confidence, food safety, and regulatory compliance.

The increasing number of packaged food products available in retail stores has made halal verification more challenging for Muslim consumers. While many products display halal certification logos, consumers often have difficulty determining whether these certifications are genuine or issued by recognized halal certifying organizations. Existing verification methods largely rely on manual inspection, barcode scanning, or online database searches, which are often time-consuming. This study aims to develop HalalVerify: A Computer Vision System for Real-Time Ingredient Additive Parsing and Halal Logo Authentication, which utilizes computer vision, deep learning, and OCR technologies to automatically parse ingredient labels, authenticate halal logos, and lookup regional food establishment criteria through a smartphone camera.

How can a relational database index be structured within a PWA to allow consumers to query local establishments regarding their halal compliance status and authenticated product availability?
What is the performance of the proposed HalalVerify system in terms of Precision, Recall, mean Average Precision (), and Character Error Rate (CER)?
How acceptable is the HalalVerify system to consumers and halal inspectors based on the System Usability Scale (SUS)?

Purpose and Description
Muslim consumers frequently encounter uncertainty when purchasing packaged food products because verifying halal compliance often requires manual inspection of certification logos, careful examination of ingredient lists, or searching through online databases. This process becomes more difficult when ingredient labels contain scientific names or food additives that are unfamiliar to consumers. Moreover, counterfeit or unverified halal logos can further reduce consumer confidence and increase the risk of purchasing products that do not comply with halal standards.
Current halal verification methods generally rely on barcode scanners, QR code verification systems, or manual database searches. Although these methods provide useful information, they require additional user effort and cannot automatically parse chemical additive text directly from images. These limitations emphasize the need for an intelligent and automated verification system that combines computer vision with database cross-referencing.
To address these challenges, the proponents propose HalalVerify: A Computer Vision System for Real-Time Ingredient Additive Parsing and Halal Logo Authentication. The system utilizes YOLOv8-Nano to detect accredited halal certification logos and EasyOCR to extract alphanumeric ingredient text and E-numbers from product packaging. Parsed text is dynamically evaluated against a custom relational backend database maintained on Supabase. Additionally, it integrates an administrative search asset enabling consumers to dynamically look up local food spots and track their current certified parameters. Verification results are presented through a user-friendly Progressive Web Application that classifies products as Halal, Haram, or Doubtful/Unverified. By automating the verification process, HalalVerify provides consumers with faster and more reliable access to halal information while supporting retailers and regulatory authorities in promoting food safety and compliance.

Scope and Limitation
This study focuses on the design, development, and evaluation of HalalVerify: A Computer Vision System for Real-Time Ingredient Additive Parsing and Halal Logo Authentication. The proposed system is an installable Progressive Web Application (PWA) optimized for standard mobile smartphones. The system provides three primary operational modules: the Ingredient Additive Parser, the Halal Logo Authenticator, and the Establishment Directory Search. The Ingredient Additive Parser allows users to scan or upload an image of a packaging label, after which EasyOCR extracts raw text strings and E-numbers to cross-reference them against chemical additive safety records in a custom Supabase relational database. Based on the parsed results, the product is classified as Halal, Haram, or Doubtful/Unverified. The Halal Logo Authenticator utilizes a fine-tuned YOLOv8-Nano object detection model to isolate and identify recognized Halal certification marks on product packaging, cross-referencing visual logo features against accredited certifying bodies stored in the Supabase backend registry. Finally, the Establishment Directory Search features a text and category-driven lookup tool allowing users to query local dining and retail establishments within Zamboanga City to verify their active compliance standing or view authenticated product listings.
The primary target users of the system are Muslim consumers, food retailers, restaurant owners, and halal regulatory inspectors. The technical stack utilizes YOLOv8-Nano, EasyOCR, and OpenCV for computer vision processing; Python with FastAPI or Flask for backend services; React.js and Tailwind CSS for the Progressive Web Application frontend; and Supabase (PostgreSQL) for database management. Technical benchmark testing will be conducted using a dataset of approximately 300–500 packaged grocery product label images collected from select retail stores within Zamboanga City.
However, the scope of this study is bound by several specific technical and operational limitations. Regarding database integration and external registry disconnection, the system operates strictly as an independent decision-support tool and does not connect via live APIs to external third-party databases, government registries, or official certifying bodies, such as the Islamic Da'wah Council of the Philippines (IDCP). All logo credentials and additive classification rules are cross-referenced exclusively against a custom-curated relational database hosted on Supabase. Consequently, while the system's logo detection module utilizes YOLOv8-Nano to identify and localize visual features of recognized Halal marks, computer vision alone cannot determine whether a detected logo is a high-quality visual counterfeit or fraudulent print without direct verification against a live official national or international registry API.
Furthermore, the system's performance is sensitive to environmental and image capture conditions. Detection and OCR extraction accuracy depend on lighting, camera angles, focus, and resolution, meaning system performance may degrade when processing wrinkled, damaged, partially obstructed, or poorly lit packaging labels. All generated system outputs are advisory and informational only, serving as decision-support tools rather than legal substitutes for formal verification by accredited halal certifying bodies. Additionally, the study explicitly excludes the verification of raw or fresh meat products, kitchen cross-contamination tracking, and restaurant food preparation methods. The system also relies on active internet connectivity for real-time database lookups unless a cached local dictionary has been successfully bundled within the browser service worker. Finally, due to strict project execution timelines, usability testing using the System Usability Scale (SUS) will involve a targeted respondent group of approximately 30 to 50 participants.
Significance of Study
The development of HalalVerify introduces an automated computer vision approach to consumer protection. By automating the detection of halal certification logos and extracting ingredient additive information using only a smartphone camera, the system reduces the need for manual dictionary searching and enables consumers to make faster, more confident purchasing decisions.
The proposed system provides significant benefits to Muslim consumers by simplifying the process of verifying halal food products. Instead of manually interpreting complex scientific ingredient labels or researching unfamiliar food additives (E-numbers), users obtain structured verification results within seconds.
For food retailers and restaurant owners, the system serves as an additional verification mechanism to ensure that products offered to customers align with halal requirements, enhancing business transparency and strengthening consumer trust.
The study is also beneficial to halal regulatory agencies and inspectors by providing a portable decision-support tool during preliminary field audits. Although the system does not replace official certification procedures, it offers a rapid method for identifying non-compliant or doubtful ingredient profiles that may require further investigation.
From an academic perspective, the study contributes to the research domain of applied computer vision, optical character recognition, and mobile consumer protection. It demonstrates how deep learning models such as YOLOv8-Nano and EasyOCR can be seamlessly integrated with a custom database engine to address real-world food transparency challenges. The project directly aligns with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 9: Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure and SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production.

Summary
The reviewed literature demonstrates that Artificial Intelligence, Computer Vision, YOLO-based object detection, and Optical Character Recognition (OCR) are highly effective technologies for automating food recognition, product label detection, and textual information parsing. Previous studies have proven that lightweight deep learning models can accurately identify food items, isolate packaging attributes, and extract complex ingredient strings while maintaining computational efficiency on standard mobile devices. However, existing platforms focus predominantly on general food classification, retail inventory tracking, or basic nutritional and allergen auditing. They lack a unified consumer protection framework specifically tailored to Halal compliance verification. To address this research gap, HalalVerify: A Computer Vision System for Real-Time Ingredient Additive Parsing and Halal Logo Authentication integrates YOLOv8-Nano for Halal logo localization, EasyOCR for ingredient text extraction, a custom Supabase relational database for E-number and additive cross-matching, and a local Establishment Directory Search into a single Progressive Web Application (PWA).
Synthesis
The reviewed literature confirms that Artificial Intelligence, Computer Vision, Object Detection, and Optical Character Recognition (OCR) have substantially advanced the automation of food recognition, label analysis, and data extraction. Light-weight deep learning architectures such as MobileNetV2 enable efficient visual classification, while YOLO object detection models provide fast, bounding-box localization of packaging features. Concurrently, OCR frameworks have proven reliable for extracting fine-grained ingredient text and additive codes, enabling consumers to make informed dietary decisions.
Despite these technological advancements, existing systems remain fragmented. Current applications focus either on general food item recognition, automated warehouse inventory parsing, or broad allergen tracking. None of the reviewed systems offer a comprehensive, edge-optimized solution that integrates real-time Halal logo detection, scientific E-number additive parsing, custom relational database cross-matching, and a localized establishment directory into a single user-centric platform. Furthermore, existing implementations rarely address volatile data handling for privacy assurance or offline-first PWA caching for low-connectivity environments.
Based on these findings, the proponents developed HalalVerify: A Computer Vision System for Real-Time Ingredient Additive Parsing and Halal Logo Authentication to address the identified research gap. The proposed system integrates YOLOv8-Nano for halal logo detection, EasyOCR for ingredient extraction. By combining these technologies, the system provides a comprehensive, real-time, and user-friendly solution for verifying halal product labels and certificates. Unlike previous studies that focus on individual technologies or specific applications, HalalVerify offers an integrated approach that enhances consumer protection, supports regulatory compliance, and contributes to the advancement of intelligent halal verification systems.

TECHNICAL BACKGROUND
HalalVerify is an installable, Progressive Web Application (PWA) designed to automate consumer safety workflows by providing real-time verification of packaged food product labels and establishment compliance standings. The system captures and analyzes visual product attributes through standard mobile smartphone camera interfaces, processing image matrices using a streamlined machine learning pipeline composed of a fine-tuned YOLOv8-Nano object detection model and an EasyOCR text parsing engine. The resulting verification and compliance data are delivered through a responsive PWA framework and displayed on an interactive dashboard that can be securely accessed by Muslim consumers, food retailers, restaurant owners, and regulatory inspectors.
The platform follows a structured Input-Process-Output (IPO) model embedded within a decentralized three-tier architecture. The Input stage consists of live camera feeds or uploaded images of packaged ingredient labels, along with text-based query inputs targeting local dining and retail establishments in Zamboanga City. During the Process stage, incoming image matrices are preprocessed using OpenCV before being routed to the specialized machine learning engines to localize recognized Halal logo credentials or parse alphanumeric chemical additive strings and E-numbers. Crucially, all camera frames and visual inference operations occur transiently inside local volatile memory (RAM) to ensure absolute data privacy; no raw camera feeds, packaging scans, or personal user identifiers are saved to permanent storage or transmitted externally. The Output stage materializes as a dynamic advisory status display classifying items as Halal, Haram, or Doubtful/Unverified, alongside an interactive establishment profile lookup directory.
The technical design is divided into three main operational layers: Presentation, Application and Logic, and Database.
Presentation Layer
The presentation layer serves as the direct graphical user interface through which distinct stakeholder groups view and interact with the processed halal compliance states. Built using React.js and Tailwind CSS, this layer translates classification flags and lookup data into an intuitive mobile canvas designed for rapid visual parsing.
Muslim Consumers: Consumers interact with a color-coded status dashboard mounted inside the PWA browser interface. The application utilizes dynamic visual banners: Green signifies a detected and authenticated Halal logo or safe additive; Yellow flags an unverified or doubtful chemical additive (displaying warning notices, e.g., "Contains Doubtful E-Number Additives"); and Red designates a strictly prohibited chemical additive (Haram) or a blacklisted label configuration.
Food Retailers and Establishment Owners: Business operators access a secure management view to oversee their establishment directory listings. This interface provides the capability to update inventory catalogs, maintain verified product lists, and display active compliance standings in the local directory index.
Halal Regulatory Inspectors: Inspectors gain entry to a dedicated administrative dashboard layout that renders historical lookup summaries and audit logs, displaying verification statistics and compliance trends within Zamboanga City to assist in localized field operations and decision support.
Application and Logic Layer
The application and logic layer acts as the primary processing engine of HalalVerify, executing computer vision routines, character extraction pipelines, and classification logic.
Computer Vision and Detection Module: Binds directly to native smartphone camera APIs via standard browser media streams. Incoming image matrices are managed using OpenCV for spatial scaling, noise filtering, and thresholding before being routed to a fine-tuned YOLOv8-Nano network for logo bounding box localization and an EasyOCR processing queue for ingredient character mapping.
Compliance Classification Mechanism: Parses extracted text strings and E-numbers against predefined relational reference tables. The system isolates scientific chemical designations and E-number codes, cross-referencing them against a custom additive reference dictionary hosted on Supabase to determine the corresponding status level (Halal, Haram, or Doubtful/Unverified).
Backend API Framework: Developed using Python (FastAPI/Flask), this module handles microservice routing, manages JSON payloads from the PWA client, structures access control for business owners, and processes relational table lookups from the persistent storage node.  
Database Layer
The database layer acts as the centralized data store responsible for logging the curated additive lexicon, registered establishment profiles, user credentials, and administrative logs.
Data Storage: The system utilizes Supabase, an open-source PostgreSQL-based relational database infrastructure. Supabase securely manages structured relational tables containing permitted and prohibited food additives, E-number classification rules, accredited certifying body records, local retail business profiles, and system access accounts.
Data Retrieval: Leveraging Supabase’s indexed REST API framework, the database layer executes high-speed queries to deliver instant additive and establishment lookup responses to the PWA frontend, eliminating heavy processing on the client device.
User Interactions
Administrators: The one who manages the data, manually operates functions, and oversee or configures the whole system operations.
Customers: Customers: Views the displayed classification results (Halal, Haram, or Doubtful/Unverified) for scanned products, along with detected halal logo authentication status, on the dashboard.

Technology Requirements
Software Requirements
Operating Systems:
Server: A 64-bit hosting environment (such as Linux Ubuntu Server or Windows 10/11) to maintain the Python inference backend, manage API requests, and coordinate database functions.
Client: Fully cross-compatible with modern web browsers (e.g., Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Apple Safari) running across standard iOS or Android mobile smartphones via Progressive Web Application view layers.
Computer Vision and Processing Engines:
Ultralytics YOLOv8-Nano: Utilized as the primary edge-optimized deep learning model architecture for high-speed, low-latency halal logo object detection and bounding box localization.
JaidedAI EasyOCR: Deep-learning-based OCR framework responsible for text line parsing, text character extraction, and ingredient label sequence string identification.
OpenCV: Responsible for low-level image stream acquisition, image scaling, contrast thresholding, and color mask normalization operations.
Web Server:
Node.js: Used to run the server-side application, Node.js provides a robust environment for handling asynchronous operations and managing multiple client connections efficiently.
Backend Framework:
Flask / FastAPI (Python): Implemented to build lightweight server endpoints, handle asynchronous API requests, and serve real-time database modifications.
Frontend and Visualization Development Tools:
React.js: Utilized to design a highly responsive user dashboard interface with dynamic visual state modifications.
Database Management System:
Supabase: Relational database management system configured to store additive classifications, E-number records, establishment profiles, and user authentication tables.
Development and Version Control Tools:
Web Development Tools: Visual Studio Code for developing the web application using React framework with Tailwind CSS.
Visual Studio Code: The core integrated development environment (IDE) utilized for script editing and system debugging.
Git: Employed to handle source code version control and maintain collaborative code branches.
Hardware Requirements
Data Acquisition Assets:
Smartphone Camera: Built-in high-definition smartphone optical sensor (minimum 12-Megapixel resolution recommended) supporting automated focus adjustments to capture clear imagery of product wrappers and certificate headers.
Local Inference Server:
Processor (CPU): Multi-core processor (Intel Core i5 / AMD Ryzen 5 or higher recommended) to manage concurrent API service routines during backend development.
Graphics Processing Unit (GPU): Dedicated CUDA-capable NVIDIA GPU workstation layer (e.g., RTX 3060 or higher) required to perform rapid neural network model training rounds and asset annotations via Roboflow.
Memory (RAM): Minimum of 8GB to 16GB of RAM to process high-throughput data pipelines smoothly.
Storage Device: Solid-State Drive (SSD) with at least 256GB of space for application configurations and historical record databases.
Networking:
Mobile Network Data / Wi-Fi Routers: Active local internet access infrastructure configured to route HTTPS data packets between the mobile PWA client and the FastAPI backend server nodes seamlessly.
Display Monitors:
Client Screens: High-density capacitive mobile touchscreens deployed directly on consumer smartphones to render color-coded verification results, spatial search lists, and directory indices clearly.
The HalalVerify application not only automates and optimizes the label verification process but also ensures accurate, non-intrusive, and efficient authentication of displayed establishment certifications. By integrating standard smartphone camera functions with a comprehensive deep learning and character parsing backend, the system provides a highly responsive, edge-optimized software solution that directly benefits consumers, store owners, and regulatory inspectors alike. The deployment of advanced machine learning engines like YOLOv8-Nano and  EasyOCR, paired with a well-structured three-tier local architecture, ensures that the application is both technologically innovative and operationally relevant, addressing the immediate transparency challenges of globalized retail and food service environments while paving the way for future advancements in intelligent consumer protection systems.
